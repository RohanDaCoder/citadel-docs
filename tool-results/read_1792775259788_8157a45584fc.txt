     1→     1→# SOURCE: Blocks and terrain
     2→     2→
     3→     3→A build that a script writes is a schematic with no file:
     4→     4→```luau
     5→     5→local road = world.build(function(b)
     6→     6→    b:box(vec3(-4, 0, -48), vec3(4, 0, 48), "gray_concrete")
     7→     7→    b:line(vec3(0, 0, -48), vec3(0, 0, 48), "white_concrete")
     8→     8→    b:block(vec3(0, 1, 0), "sea_lantern")     -- later writes win
     9→     9→end)
    10→    10→road.size, road.min          -- vec3(9, 2, 97) and vec3(-4, 0, -48)
    11→    11→world.paste(road, vec3(0, 64, 0))             -- road.min lands here
    12→    12→```
    13→    13→The rules:
    14→    14→- Coordinates are whole blocks in the build’s own space, and may be
    15→    15→negative. A build written around `vec3(0, 0, 0)` lands with its middle
    16→    16→`-build.min` from `origin`.
    17→    17→- A block name this game version lacks becomes the nearest block it has.
    18→    18→- `chunk:paste` and `world.paste` take a build like any schematic. Make it
    19→    19→at the top of the script, not inside `on_generate`.
    20→    20→- A build costs only what it writes. One build may write 4 096 16-block
    21→    21→cubes (32 MB, sixteen million solid blocks), in a box of up to 256 million
    22→    22→blocks. For more, use several builds or a `.schem`.
    23→    23→- Fences, walls, glass panes and iron bars join their neighbours, and stairs
    24→    24→take their corners. Write `"oak_fence"`, not its `north=true` states.
    25→    25→## Player heads
    26→    26→A `"player_head"` or `"player_wall_head"` shows a face when you give it a
    27→    27→`skin`:
    28→    28→```luau
    29→    29→world.set_block(vec3(0, 65, 0), "player_head[rotation=8]", { skin = "Notch" })
    30→    30→world.set_block(vec3(1, 65, 0), "player_wall_head[facing=south]", { skin = player.name })
    31→    31→world.set_block(vec3(2, 65, 0), "player_head", { skin = { value = "ewogICJ0aW1l...", signature = "..." } })
    32→    32→world.set_blocks({ { at = vec3(3, 65, 0), block = "player_head", skin = "jeb_" } })
    33→    33→```
    34→    34→- A name is looked up from a player in this room, else from Mojang. The
    35→    35→head appears at once and gets its face later. A name with no account
    36→    36→leaves a plain head and logs a line.
    37→    37→- `{ value = , signature = }` is the account’s `textures` property, as for
    38→    38→`world.npc{ skin = }`. The signature is optional.
    39→    39→- The face is saved with the chunk. Replacing, breaking or turning the head
    40→    40→forgets it: write the new state with its `skin` again.
    41→    41→- A head in a `.schem` (Sponge or MCEdit) or an imported world keeps its
    42→    42→face through pastes, turns and `citadel import`. A head saved with only a
    43→    43→name is looked up when its chunk loads.
    44→    44→- `skin` on any other block is an error.
    45→    45→- Clients from 1.7 see the face. Bedrock clients see no face.
    46→    46→- For a head as an item, see “head items”.
    47→    47→## Half blocks: `detail = 2`
    48→    48→At `detail = 2`, every coordinate is a half block. The engine turns each
    49→    49→block’s eight cells into the block, a slab or a stair of the material, with
    50→    50→the correct `facing`, `half` and `shape`:
    51→    51→```luau
    52→    52→local roof = world.build(function(b)
    53→    53→    for k = 0, 9 do                          -- a 45-degree roof, half a block a step
    54→    54→        b:box(vec3(k, k, 0), vec3(19 - k, k, 15), "deepslate_tiles")
    55→    55→    end
    56→    56→end, { detail = 2 })                         -- 10 blocks wide: coordinates 0..19
    57→    57→```
    58→    58→| cells of a block | what is placed |
    59→    59→| --- | --- |
    60→    60→| all eight | the block |
    61→    61→| the bottom four, or the top four | a bottom or top slab |
    62→    62→| a half and one quarter above it (or below) | an outer-corner stair |
    63→    63→| a half and two quarters on one side | a straight stair, climbing that side |
    64→    64→| a half and three quarters | an inner-corner stair |
    65→    65→| anything else | the block when four or more cells hold it, else nothing |
    66→    66→- The slab and the stair are the material’s own: `stone_bricks` makes
    67→    67→`stone_brick_slab` and `stone_brick_stairs`, `oak_planks` makes
    68→    68→`oak_slab`, `quartz_block` makes `quartz_stairs`.
    69→    69→- A material with no slab or stairs, or whose slab or stairs came in a
    70→    70→later release (concrete’s), is the whole block or nothing.
    71→    71→- Only full blocks are fitted. A lantern, flower, fence or stair named
    72→    72→directly is placed as named in a block with no full-block cells. So a
    73→    73→detail is one `b:block`, not eight.
    74→    74→- A block that holds two materials takes the one most of its cells hold.
    75→    75→- `detail` is `1` (the default) or `2`. `build.size` and `build.min` are in
    76→    76→blocks. The cube limit counts cells, so detail 2 holds an eighth as many
    77→    77→blocks.
    78→    78→## Turning a build
    79→    79→A build turns and flips as a value; the original does not change:
    80→    80→- A build turns about `vec3(0, 0, 0)`, so a part drawn around its middle
    81→    81→turns in place. `min` reads back the new start.
    82→    82→- Block states turn with it: facings, log axes, fence and pane sides, stair
    83→    83→corners, door hinges, rail curves, sign and banner rotation.
    84→    84→- `rotate` takes 90, 180 or 270. Any other value is an error.
    85→    85→## Cutting a build, and changing its blocks
    86→    86→A build somebody made by hand is a kit of pieces. `crop` takes one piece out
    87→    87→of it, and `replace` makes it of other blocks. Both give a new build; the
    88→    88→original does not change.
    89→    89→- The corners are in the build’s own blocks, in either order. A file’s
    90→    90→lowest corner is `vec3(0, 0, 0)`.
    91→    91→- A box that reaches outside the build is cut to it. A box with none of the
    92→    92→build in it is an error that says where the build runs.
    93→    93→- A cropped piece has no named parts. `replace` keeps them.
    94→    94→- `replace` takes block names with no state. A block keeps its state where
    95→    95→the new block has one like it: stairs stay stairs that face the same way.
    96→    96→Where it has none, the block is the plain block. `"air"` takes a block
    97→    97→away.
    98→    98→- A name that is not a block is an error that names it.
    99→    99→- To save memory, crop the pieces while the pack loads and keep only them:
   100→   100→a file nobody holds is let go.
   101→   101→## Naming the parts
   102→   102→A build names its pieces as it writes them, and the names stay after the
   103→   103→paste. `citadel render` frames a part by name. `citadel check` says which
   104→   104→part an edit touched.
   105→   105→```luau
   106→   106→local house = world.build(function(b)
   107→   107→    b:part("walls", function()
   108→   108→        b:box(vec3(-5, 0, -4), vec3(5, 4, 4), "stone_bricks")
   109→   109→        b:part("door", function() ... end)      -- "walls/door"
   110→   110→    end)
   111→   111→    b:part("roof", function() ... end)
   112→   112→end, { detail = 2 })
   113→   113→world.paste(house, vec3(0, 64, 0), { part = "house" })   -- "house", "house/walls", "house/roof"
   114→   114→chunk:paste(house, vec3(0, 64, 0), { part = "house" })   -- the same from on_generate
   115→   115→```
   116→   116→- A part is the box its writes reached, in whole blocks at either detail.
   117→   117→It turns and flips with the build.
   118→   118→- A name is 1 to 64 characters without a `/`. A part inside another part is
   119→   119→named by its path, `"walls/door"`.
   120→   120→- A name may repeat. Selecting it takes every part with that name.
   121→   121→- Name the parts you will talk about: the body, each tower or wing, the
   122→   122→roof, the entrance. Then “make the tower taller” stays an edit to the
   123→   123→tower.
   124→   124→## Seeing what you built
   125→   125→`citadel render <pack dir> --out view.png` runs the pack with nobody joining
   126→   126→and draws its world.
   127→   127→- Each block has its real shape, in the average colour of its textures.
   128→   128→- With named parts, four cameras frame them from the sides (`view-1.png`
   129→   129→looks north). Otherwise they circle the spawn.
   130→   130→Ask for the view that answers your question:
   131→   131→```sh
   132→   132→citadel render ./castle --out v.png --view approach,entrance     # does the way in read?
   133→   133→citadel render ./castle --out v.png --view roof --part castle/keep
   134→   134→citadel render ./castle --out v.png --view plan,cutaway          # the enclosed spaces, storey by storey
   135→   135→citadel render ./castle --out gate.png --at 0,66,30 --look 0,70,-10   # one camera, placed
   136→   136→```
   137→   137→| view | camera | the question it answers |
   138→   138→| --- | --- | --- |
   139→   139→| `sides` | four, round the build, a little above it | silhouette and proportion from every side |
   140→   140→| `approach` | a player’s eye height, out from the main door | what a player sees walking up to it |
   141→   141→| `entrance` | close on the main door, eye height | does the way in read as the way in |
   142→   142→| `roof` | high up, at an angle | the roof line, and where the parts meet |
   143→   143→| `plan` | straight down, to scale, one per storey | the enclosed spaces, walls and doors of each floor |
   144→   144→| `cutaway` | at an angle, one per storey | the enclosed spaces as a player stands in them |
   145→   145→| `eye` | in each named part, where a player stands, at eye height | the part as one who walks in sees it: a hall from inside, a square from its edge |
   146→   146→| `night` | as `sides`, at night | the light: only blocks that give light (lanterns, lamps, glowstone, froglights) light the build |
   147→   147→- `--part name` frames that part and the parts inside it.
   148→   148→`--from x,y,z --to x,y,z` frames a box. Without either, `--view` frames
   149→   149→every named part.
   150→   150→- The main door is the lowest door that opens outdoors. A build with no
   151→   151→`*_door` block has no `approach` or `entrance` view.
   152→   152→- `plan` and `cutaway` remove everything above each storey. `--cut y`
   153→   153→removes everything above `y` for any view. `--night` draws any view at
   154→   154→night, the placed cameras of `--at` too.
   155→   155→- Under a roof, a face is lit by the sky light that comes in through the
   156→   156→openings and by the light blocks near it, as in the game: a room with
   157→   157→windows is light, one without is dark.
   158→   158→- `--around x,y,z`, `--distance d`, `--height h` and `--views n` circle a
   159→   159→point instead. `--size 1280x720`, `--fov 60` and `--radius r` (how far
   160→   160→around the target the world is read) change the rest.
   161→   161→- Each file prints one JSON line on stdout: `{"file":..., "view":..., "eye":[x,y,z], "look":[x,y,z]}`, with `"cut"` when something was removed.
   162→   162→**What a picture proves.** Shape, depth, proportion and silhouette, not
   163→   163→textures: every block is one flat colour. Say so when you report on how a
   164→   164→build looks. At night a block is lit by the light blocks within 15 blocks of
   165→   165→it, as in the game; a lamp outside the views’ box lights nothing.
   166→   166→## Checking what you built
   167→   167→`citadel check <pack dir>` measures the named build and reports what is
   168→   168→wrong with it:
   169→   169→```sh
   170→   170→citadel check ./castle                          # every named part
   171→   171→citadel check ./castle --part castle/keep --storeys 3 --enclosed
   172→   172→citadel check ./castle --save before.snap       # write the blocks down ...
   173→   173→citadel check ./castle --since before.snap      # ... and say what changed, part by part
   174→   174→```
   175→   175→It lists:
   176→   176→- the enclosed spaces (the air closed in with doors and windows shut)
   177→   177→- how many blocks the build holds
   178→   178→- the storeys
   179→   179→- for each innermost part: its places to stand and the detail near them
   180→   180→(blocks within reach that are not a full cube and not glass)
   181→   181→- the doors, and where each opens
   182→   182→- each face: how much is one plane, its windows and how many are framed,
   183→   183→its materials
   184→   184→- the roof: how much is one height, how much is stairs and slabs
   185→   185→- the setting: how much of the ground around it has something placed.
   186→   186→Then it lists the findings:
   187→   187→| finding | kind | what it means |
   188→   188→| --- | --- | --- |
   189→   189→| `FLOATING` | fix | blocks that touch nothing standing |
   190→   190→| `DOOR_BLOCKED` | fix | a block in a door’s way at foot or head height, named |
   191→   191→| `DOOR_DROP` | fix | a door that opens onto nothing to stand on |
   192→   192→| `DOOR_UNREACHED` | fix | a door nobody can walk to from outside |
   193→   193→| `UNREACHED` | fix | floor inside walls and under a roof that nobody can walk to from outside |
   194→   194→| `STOREYS` | fix | `--storeys n` asked for, and not what enclosed spaces stand on |
   195→   195→| `NOT_CLOSED_IN` | fix with `--enclosed` | outside air reaches the inside with doors and windows shut, and where it comes in |
   196→   196→| `FLAT_FACE` | advice | a face nearly all in one plane |
   197→   197→| `FLUSH_WINDOWS` | advice | windows with nothing in front of them: no sill, lintel or post that stands out |
   198→   198→| `ONE_MATERIAL` | advice | a face nearly all one block |
   199→   199→| `FLAT_ROOF` | advice | the top one flat height, not stepped |
   200→   200→| `BLOCKY_ROOF` | advice | a sloping roof of whole blocks where stairs could be |
   201→   201→| `LOW_CEILING` | advice | a room two blocks high |
   202→   202→| `NO_SETTING` | advice | bare ground all round |
   203→   203→| `BARE_FACE` | advice | a face with few shape blocks (stairs, slabs, walls, trapdoors, lanterns): masses without detail |
   204→   204→| `DARK_AT_NIGHT` | advice | over half of the places a player walks to is dark at night |
   205→   205→| `SPARSE` | advice | a part where players walk with little detail near them: an empty hall, square or deck |
   206→   206→| `PART_OVERLAP` | advice | two parts (`parts.define`) overlap without one inside the other: regenerating either rewrites the other |
   207→   207→- A fix is wrong for any build. Weigh advice against the build’s purpose:
   208→   208→a market stall is open, and a tower’s roof may be flat.
   209→   209→- A player walks on two clear cells over something solid, steps onto stairs
   210→   210→and slabs, jumps one block, drops three, and climbs ladders and vines.
   211→   211→Blocks are sorted by their names.
   212→   212→- `--json` prints one JSON object. The command exits 1 when there is
   213→   213→something to fix.
   214→   214→- `--since` counts changed blocks by the innermost part that holds them,
   215→   215→and counts the rest as `(in no part)`.
   216→   216→Ship a build too big for `chunk:set` lines as a `.schem` in the pack folder,
   217→   217→and paste it:
   218→   218→```luau
   219→   219→local lobby = world.schematic("builds/lobby.schem")   -- at the top, not per chunk
   220→   220→world.on_generate(function(chunk)
   221→   221→    chunk:paste(lobby, vec3(0, 64, 0))         -- origin is the build's lowest corner
   222→   222→end)
   223→   223→world.paste(lobby, vec3(100, 64, 0))           -- the same build, live
   224→   224→chunk:paste(lobby, vec3(0, 64, 0), { air = true })   -- both take this third argument
   225→   225→```
   226→   226→The rules:
   227→   227→- `file` is a path inside the pack folder, written with `/`. These are
   228→   228→errors that name the file: a path from the root, a path that leaves the
   229→   229→pack with `..`, a missing file, and a file that is not a schematic.
   230→   230→- Call `world.schematic` while the pack loads and keep the value. Inside
   231→   231→`on_generate`, it reads the file again for every chunk.
   232→   232→- `origin` is where the build’s **lowest** corner goes. The build fills
   233→   233→`origin` to `origin + schem.size - vec3(1, 1, 1)`.
   234→   234→- Air in the build is skipped, so a paste sits on the terrain. Pass
   235→   235→`{ air = true }` to place the air too.
   236→   236→- `chunk:paste` writes only the part inside its chunk, so the call in
   237→   237→`on_generate` builds the whole thing. `world.paste` writes all of it at
   238→   238→once, like `world.set_blocks`.
   239→   239→- Sponge v2, Sponge v3 and the old MCEdit `.schematic` all load. Block
   240→   240→entities (chest contents, signs) and entities do not: a chest pastes
   241→   241→empty.
   242→   242→- A paste does not turn. Turn the value first with `:rotate` or `:mirror`
   243→   243→(see “turning a build”). A file’s schematic has them too.
   244→   244→- A paste keeps the exact blocks of any release Citadel knows. A newer
   245→   245→name becomes the nearest block. What nothing covers becomes air, and the
   246→   246→log names and counts it once per file.
   247→   247→## The time of day
   248→   248→The time is in ticks, 0 at dawn and 24000 to a day. Each room has its own
   249→   249→time, and late joiners get the same sky.
   250→   250→```luau
   251→   251→world.time("night")          -- sets the hour: sunrise 23000, day 1000, noon 6000, sunset 12000, night 13000, midnight 18000
   252→   252→world.time(18000)            -- sets the hour, in ticks
   253→   253→world.time(function(s)       -- a cycle: asked each tick, s seconds after this call
   254→   254→    return 1000 + s * 40     -- a day of ten minutes, from morning
   255→   255→end)
   256→   256→world.time(function(s)       -- anything the pack likes: a day that stops at dusk
   257→   257→    return math.min(1000 + s * 20, 12000)
   258→   258→end)
   259→   259→local now = world.time()     -- 0 .. 23999
   260→   260→```
   261→   261→- A call replaces the previous one, a function too.
   262→   262→- The function returns ticks or a name. If it errors, the sky stays where
   263→   263→it was.
   264→   264→- Whether the sun moves is a rule: `world.rules{ day_cycle = true }` runs
   265→   265→the version’s own day (20 minutes), and nothing else of survival.
   266→   266→- On a `mode: lite` world and in a room made by `rooms.create`, the rule is
   267→   267→off and the sun stays straight overhead (noon).
   268→   268→- On a `mode: full` world, and after `world.rules{ survival = true }`, the
   269→   269→rule is on.
   270→   270→- A number or a name sets the hour. With `day_cycle` on, the day runs on
   271→   271→from there, as `/time set` does. With it off, the sky stays there.
   272→   272→- A function drives the sky itself. The engine’s day stands aside until the
   273→   273→next `world.time` call.
   274→   274→- A stopped sky, the normal day or an `s * 20` day costs one packet a second. A faster or
   275→   275→slower day costs one packet every tick it moves.
   276→   276→Reading blocks:
   277→   277→**A read never generates.** A position in an unloaded chunk reads as `nil`
   278→   278→(not loaded), not `"air"`. Inside `on_generate`, `chunk:get` always works.
   279→   279→A block change is stored, so late joiners see it. A block name may list `|`
   280→   280→alternatives (`"deepslate|stone"`): the first one the game version knows
   281→   281→wins. Without `|`, an unknown name becomes the nearest block, never air.
   282→   282→## Containers
   283→   283→A chest’s stacks, for a pipe, a quarry or a sorting machine to take from
   284→   284→and put into:
   285→   285→- A chest a player has open belongs to that player until they close it.
   286→   286→Until then, `take` and `put` do nothing.
   287→   287→- Each half of a double chest is its own 27 slots.
   288→   288→- A kept handle never goes stale. `items()` on a chest that is gone returns
   289→   289→an empty list.
   290→   290→## Populating the overworld
   291→   291→`on_populate` works on the engine’s overworld (`world: overworld`, or
   292→   292→`mode: full` with no `world` key). It gets each new chunk with terrain,
   293→   293→caves and ores in place, with the same `chunk:get`, `chunk:set` and
   294→   294→`chunk:fill` as `on_generate`. Use it for your own ores and trees. A changed
   295→   295→hook reaches new land only.
   296→   296→## Biomes
   297→   297→A biome is what the client colours a column by: the grass, the leaves, the
   298→   298→water, the sky and the fog. It is also the weather there. Each block column
   299→   299→has one biome.
   300→   300→- A column that nothing sets is `"plains"`.
   301→   301→- The engine’s overworld sets the biomes of its own chunks. `on_populate`
   302→   302→can read them and change them.
   303→   303→- A name is a vanilla biome name of any release: `"windswept_hills"` and
   304→   304→`"mountains"` are the same hill. A wrong name is an error.
   305→   305→- A client that lacks a biome shows the nearest one it has. A 1.8 client
   306→   306→shows `"cherry_grove"` as plains.
   307→   307→A pack makes biomes of its own. A new biome is a copy of a vanilla biome
   308→   308→with other colours and weather:
   309→   309→```luau
   310→   310→local crystal = game.biomes.define {
   311→   311→    name = "crystal",        -- becomes "mypack:crystal", which define returns
   312→   312→    like = "forest",         -- the vanilla biome to copy; "plains" when left out
   313→   313→    grass = "#7fd8c8",       -- each colour is a colour name or a color value
   314→   314→    leaves = "#58b7a6",
   315→   315→    water = "#3fd0ff",
   316→   316→    water_fog = "#0a4060",   -- the view under water
   317→   317→    sky = "#b0e8ff",
   318→   318→    fog = "#e0f6ff",         -- the far distance
   319→   319→    weather = "snow",        -- "rain" or "snow"; "none" or false for no weather
   320→   320→}
   321→   321→
   322→   322→world.on_generate(function(chunk)
   323→   323→    chunk:fill_biome(chunk.min, chunk.max, crystal)
   324→   324→end)
   325→   325→```
   326→   326→- Define biomes at the top of the script. A client learns the biomes when
   327→   327→it joins.
   328→   328→- Clients from 1.16.2 show a new biome. An older client and a Bedrock
   329→   329→client show the `like` biome.
   330→   330→- A changed colour reaches a player on the next join. A biome that is
   331→   331→new after the first player joined shows as its `like` until the server
   332→   332→restarts. The console says so.
   333→   333→- A colour that is left out stays as `like` has it. `weather = "snow"`
   334→   334→also makes the grass and leaves colder in colour, unless you set them.
   335→   335→- A biome does not make trees or blocks. The generator puts them there.
   336→   336→## Noise
   337→   337→`noise` gives smooth random numbers for hills, caves and biome borders. The
   338→   338→engine calculates them, so a generator stays inside its 100 ms.
   339→   339→| Option | Default | Meaning |
   340→   340→| --- | --- | --- |
   341→   341→| `seed` | 0 | the same seed gives the same numbers each time |
   342→   342→| `scale` | 64 | the size of one hill, in blocks |
   343→   343→| `octaves` | 1 | layers of detail, 1 to 16 |
   344→   344→| `lacunarity` | 2 | how many times finer each next layer is |
   345→   345→| `gain` | 0.5 | how many times as strong each next layer is |
   346→   346→A world with hills and two biomes:
   347→   347→```luau
   348→   348→local hills = noise.new { seed = 7, scale = 96, octaves = 4 }
   349→   349→local climate = noise.new { seed = 8, scale = 300 }
   350→   350→
   351→   351→world.on_generate(function(chunk)
   352→   352→    local h = hills:grid(chunk.min.x, chunk.min.z, 16, 16)
   353→   353→    local c = climate:grid(chunk.min.x, chunk.min.z, 16, 16)
   354→   354→    for z = 0, 15 do
   355→   355→        for x = 0, 15 do
   356→   356→            local i = z * 16 + x + 1
   357→   357→            local at = chunk.min + vec3(x, 0, z)
   358→   358→            local top = 64 + math.floor(h[i] * 24)
   359→   359→            local dry = c[i] > 0
   360→   360→            chunk:fill(at, at + vec3(0, top - 1, 0), "stone")
   361→   361→            chunk:set(at + vec3(0, top, 0), dry and "sand" or "grass_block")
   362→   362→            chunk:set_biome(at, dry and "desert" or "forest")
   363→   363→        end
   364→   364→    end
   365→   365→end)
   366→   366→```
   367→   367→- Make each noise at the top of the script, not inside `on_generate`.
   368→   368→- Ask for a chunk with one `grid` call. 256 `get` calls are slower.
   369→   369→- `grid` gives up to 65 536 numbers (256 x 256).
   370→   370→## A declared world
   371→   371→`world.generate` declares a world as rules. The engine makes each chunk from
   372→   372→them, on all cores, and no script runs for a chunk. Use it for a large world.
   373→   373→`world.on_generate` stays for what the rules cannot say.
   374→   374→```luau
   375→   375→local hills = fields.noise { seed = 7, scale = 96, octaves = 4 }
   376→   376→local dry = fields.noise { seed = 8, scale = 300 }:gt(0)
   377→   377→
   378→   378→local made = world.generate {
   379→   379→    height = 64 + hills * 24,                -- the y of the surface
   380→   380→    sea = 62,                                -- water fills the open air up to here
   381→   381→    biomes = { { "desert", when = dry }, { "forest" } },   -- the first that holds
   382→   382→    surface = {                              -- the first rule that holds, for each block
   383→   383→        { block = "stone", when = fields.slope:gt(2), depth = 4 },
   384→   384→        { block = "sand", biome = "desert", depth = 4 },
   385→   385→        { block = "grass_block" },           -- depth 1: the top block
   386→   386→        { block = "dirt", depth = 4 },       -- the 3 blocks below it
   387→   387→    },
   388→   388→    rock = { { block = "stone" } },          -- the body of the land
   389→   389→    plants = { { block = "short_grass", chance = 0.3, biome = "forest" } },
   390→   390→    things = { { cell = 8, { shapes = { oak }, chance = 0.4, biome = "forest" } } },
   391→   391→}
   392→   392→made:sample(hills, pos)     -- a field's value there, as the engine calculates it
   393→   393→made:biome_at(pos)          -- "forest"
   394→   394→```
   395→   395→**A field** is a number at each block, written as a recipe. `fields.noise`
   396→   396→gives one, and maths on fields gives more: `+ - * / ^`, a minus sign, and
   397→   397→the methods. A number is a field too.
   398→   398→```luau
   399→   399→-- 1 on a column with water beside it: sugar cane beside water.
   400→   400→local w = fields.water
   401→   401→local beside_water = w:at(1, 0, 0):max(w:at(-1, 0, 0)):max(w:at(0, 0, 1)):max(w:at(0, 0, -1)):gt(0.5)
   402→   402→plants = { { block = "sugar_cane", height = 2, chance = 0.5, when = beside_water * w:lt(0.5) } }
   403→   403→```
   404→   404→- A rule asks a question with `when`: it holds where the field is above
   405→   405→0.5. `a * b` is “and”, `a:max(b)` is “or”, `1 - a` is “not”.
   406→   406→- `height` cannot use `fields.height`, `slope`, `water` or `shore`: they
   407→   407→are read from it.
   408→   408→- `biome` in a rule is a biome name. It is the same as a `when`, and faster.
   409→   409→**The rules:**
   410→   410→| Key | A rule | Meaning |
   411→   411→| --- | --- | --- |
   412→   412→| `biomes` | `{ "desert", when = f }` | the first that holds names the biome of the column |
   413→   413→| `surface` | `{ block, biome, when, depth }` | `block` for the top `depth` blocks (1) of each surface |
   414→   414→| `rock` | the same | the body of the land under the surface rules |
   415→   415→| `plants` | `{ block, chance, biome, when, ... }` | at most one plant on a column |
   416→   416→| `things` | `{ cell, seed, { shapes, chance, biome, when, afloat, place, y }, ... }` | at most one thing in a grid cell |
   417→   417→| `fluids` | `{ block, below, biome, when }` | the carved air under `below` becomes `block`; the first that holds |
   418→   418→- A **block** is a name, or a list with a field that chooses:
   419→   419→`{ "stone", "andesite", by = fields.hash(3) * 2 }`. `floor(by)` counts
   420→   420→through the list.
   421→   421→- A **plant** is `block`, or `blocks = { lower, upper }` for a plant of two
   422→   422→blocks. With `height = f` it grows: `block` repeats, and `tip` is the top
   423→   423→block. `afloat = true` puts it on the water, and `sink = 1` puts it one
   424→   424→block lower (ice in the top of the sea). `clear = n` keeps `n` blocks of
   425→   425→water free over a plant that grows in water.
   426→   426→- A column tries the plant rules in order. Each rule that holds takes its
   427→   427→`chance` of the column’s roll, so two rules of 0.3 and 0.2 give a plant
   428→   428→on half of the columns.
   429→   429→- A plant stays only where the game version’s own rule lets it stay, for
   430→   430→example cane needs water beside the block under it, at the same level.
   431→   431→Where the rule fails, the column has no plant. Light is not known yet,
   432→   432→so a rule about light is not checked.
   433→   433→- A **thing** is one of `shapes`: builds from `world.build` or
   434→   434→`world.schematic`. Its `(0, 0, 0)` goes on the block above the ground. A
   435→   435→thing that crosses a chunk border is complete. Nothing stands on ground
   436→   436→that is under water, but `afloat = true` puts the thing on the water.
   437→   437→- `density = f` makes a block solid where `f` is above 0. Use it with a
   438→   438→`solid` noise for caves, overhangs and land in the air. With `height`
   439→   439→too, a block must pass both.
   440→   440→- Without `height` and `density`, the rules add to the land that the
   441→   441→engine’s own terrain made (`world: overworld`): surface rules do not
   442→   442→run, plants grow on each column, and a thing must stand inside one chunk.
   443→   443→- Make the shapes and the fields at the top of the script, within the load
   444→   444→limits (see “loading”).
   445→   445→**Caves.** `density` carves air under the top surface. A plant or a thing
   446→   446→goes into that air with `place`, and `fluids` fill it:
   447→   447→```luau
   448→   448→local caves = fields.noise { seed = 3, scale = 24, octaves = 2, solid = true }
   449→   449→world.generate {
   450→   450→    height = 72 + hills * 20,
   451→   451→    density = 1 - caves:abs():lt(0.12) * fields.y:gt(5),   -- caves under y 5 and up
   452→   452→    rock = { { block = "stone" } },
   453→   453→    plants = {
   454→   454→        { block = "moss_carpet", place = "floor", chance = 0.6 },          -- moss on cave floors
   455→   455→        { block = "cave_vines_plant", tip = "cave_vines", height = fields.room / 3,
   456→   456→          place = "ceiling", chance = 0.3 },                               -- glow berries from the ceilings
   457→   457→        { block = "glow_lichen[{wall}=true]", place = "wall", chance = 0.1, y = { 0, 50 } },
   458→   458→    },
   459→   459→    fluids = { { block = "water", below = 30 }, { block = "lava", below = 10 } },  -- a lake, and lava under it
   460→   460→    things = { { cell = 6, { shapes = { stalagmite }, place = "floor", when = fields.room:gt(5) } } },
   461→   461→}
   462→   462→```
   463→   463→| `place` | Where | At most |
   464→   464→| --- | --- | --- |
   465→   465→| `"top"` (the default) | on the top surface, as before | one plant on a column |
   466→   466→| `"floor"` | the air block over each floor of a cave | one plant on each floor |
   467→   467→| `"ceiling"` | the air block under each ceiling, hanging down | one plant under each ceiling |
   468→   468→| `"wall"` | each air block of a cave with rock on a side (plants only) | one plant in each block |
   469→   469→- A column can cross many caves. Each floor and each ceiling of each cave
   470→   470→is its own place, with its own roll.
   471→   471→- `y = { low, high }` keeps a rule to the blocks it starts at from `low` to
   472→   472→`high`. `when`, `chance` and `y` are read at the air block the plant
   473→   473→starts in, not at the rock under it.
   474→   474→- A plant that grows (`height`) under a ceiling hangs down: `block` is at
   475→   475→the ceiling and `tip` is the lowest block. With `blocks`, the first is at
   476→   476→the ceiling. A plant never grows into rock, a fluid or another plant.
   477→   477→- A wall plant is one block. In its name, `{wall}` is the side the rock is
   478→   478→on and `{facing}` is the side away from it: `"vine[{wall}=true]"`,
   479→   479→`"wall_torch[facing={facing}]"`, `"cocoa[facing={wall}]"`.
   480→   480→- A thing on a floor has its `(0, 0, 0)` on the air block over the floor. A
   481→   481→thing under a ceiling has its `(0, 0, 0)` on the air block under the
   482→   482→ceiling, so build it down from there (negative y). A grid cell holds one
   483→   483→thing; when its column has several caves, the cell’s roll picks one. A
   484→   484→thing does not look at fluids or at the size of the cave: use
   485→   485→`when = fields.room:gt(n)` and `y` to keep it in a cave it fits.
   486→   486→- A fluid fills only carved air, from the floor of a cave up to `below - 1`.
   487→   487→The open sky over the top surface is the `sea`’s, not the fluids’. A cave
   488→   488→that opens to the sea from the side is not flooded: give it a fluid rule
   489→   489→under the sea level. Plants go only where the air stays air.
   490→   490→- `fields.room` counts the air block, the air over it and the air under
   491→   491→it, up to 64. The open sky over the top surface reads 64. Read it with
   492→   492→`at` anywhere; another column’s room costs one `density` read for each
   493→   493→block of it. `density` cannot use `fields.room`.
   494→   494→- `fields.depth` is measured from `height`, so it needs a `height`.
   495→   495→- The game version’s own plant rule holds in caves too. On 1.8.9, grass and
   496→   496→flowers need dirt under them, so a stone floor keeps none. Cave blocks
   497→   497→that 1.8.9 does not have (moss, glow lichen, hanging roots, cave vines)
   498→   498→stay. `vine` keeps its 1.8.9 rule: rock on its side.
   499→   499→- Caves need `density` or `carvers`: a rule with `place` under the top, or
   500→   500→a fluid, without one of them is an error.
   501→   501→**The game’s own caves and structures.** `carvers` and `structures` run the
   502→   502→game version’s own generators over the declared land, with the world seed:
   503→   503→```luau
   504→   504→world.generate {
   505→   505→    height = 64 + hills * 20,
   506→   506→    rock = { { block = "stone" } },
   507→   507→    carvers = { "caves", "ravines" },        -- the game's tunnels and ravines
   508→   508→    structures = { "mineshaft", "dungeon" }, -- with rails, spawners and loot
   509→   509→    plants = { { block = "moss_carpet", place = "floor" } },  -- in those caves
   510→   510→}
   511→   511→```
   512→   512→| Key | Names | Versions |
   513→   513→| --- | --- | --- |
   514→   514→| `carvers` | `"caves"` | every version |
   515→   515→|  | `"ravines"` | b1.8 and later |
   516→   516→| `structures` | `"mineshaft"`, `"dungeon"` | 1.7 to 1.12 |
   517→   517→- They dig and build after the land (`height`, `density`) and before the
   518→   518→plants, fluids and things, so the cave rules (`place`, `fluids`) use the
   519→   519→caves they dig. They need `height` or `density`: the game’s own terrain
   520→   520→(`world: overworld`) has its own.
   521→   521→- A dungeon’s spawner has its mob and its chest has its loot, as in the
   522→   522→game. Without the lists, the world has none of them.
   523→   523→- A name this version does not have is an error that names the ones it has.
